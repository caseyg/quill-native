The IndieWeb

Your website is the hub.

The IndieWeb is people publishing on sites they own, instead of on platforms that own their posts. A handful of small, open standards let those sites talk to each other and to apps like Quill. Here’s how the pieces fit.

Step by step

What happens when you post from Quill

  1. You type your domain Discovery

    Quill reads your homepage for links that say where your site signs people in and where it accepts posts. No account with Quill, and no settings to copy.

  2. Your site signs you in IndieAuth

    Quill opens your site’s sign-in page in the system browser. You approve, and your site hands Quill a token that can post for you. The token stays in your Keychain.

  3. Quill sends the post Micropub

    When you tap Post, Quill sends your words, photos and details to your site’s Micropub endpoint. Your site answers with the new post’s address.

  4. Your site publishes it Microformats

    Your site puts the post on a page, marked up with a few class names like h-entry and p-name so other sites and readers can understand it.

  5. Your site lets others know Webmention

    If you replied to, liked or linked to someone’s post, your site pings theirs. Their replies and likes come back the same way and can show up under your post.

  6. Copies go out, if you want POSSE

    Your site can syndicate a copy to social networks, linking back to the original. Pick the targets per post in Quill’s Details.

Under the hood

It’s just HTML and HTTP

Everything Quill needs is in two places: a few links in your homepage, and one request to your site.

Your homepage’s <head>
<link rel="indieauth-metadata"
  href="https://yoursite.com/auth/meta">
<link rel="micropub"
  href="https://yoursite.com/micropub">
What Quill sends
POST /micropub
Authorization: Bearer …

h=entry&content=Planted+the+tomatoes
&category[]=garden
What your site publishes
<article class="h-entry">
  <p class="e-content">Planted the tomatoes</p>
  <a class="u-url" href="/2026/11/17/tomatoes"><time class="dt-published">Nov 17</time></a>
</article>

The standards

The specs, in a sentence each

W3C Recommendation

Micropub

An API for creating, editing and deleting posts on any site. What Quill speaks.

micropub.spec.indieweb.org Living standard

IndieAuth

Sign in with your domain. Built on OAuth 2.0, with your site as the identity.

indieauth.spec.indieweb.org Community standard

Microformats

Class names that make posts, people and events in ordinary HTML readable by machines.

microformats.org W3C Recommendation

Webmention

A ping from one site to another saying “I linked to you”. How replies and likes cross sites.

w3.org/TR/webmention W3C Note

Post Type Discovery

How to tell a like from a reply from a note, by which properties a post has.

w3.org/TR/post-type-discovery W3C Recommendation

WebSub

Real-time notifications that a feed has changed, so readers don’t have to keep checking.

w3.org/TR/websub Draft

Microsub

The reading side of Micropub: one API for following feeds, so reader apps and servers can mix and match.

indieweb.org/Microsub Practice

POSSE

Publish on your Own Site, Syndicate Elsewhere. The original stays home; copies link back.

indieweb.org/POSSE
